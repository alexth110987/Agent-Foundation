## Recorded design decisions
- **AF-01 — Approved design decision:** The Master Control System may propose additional policy rules, but proposals remain non-operative until the owner explicitly approves them and the approved policy source is updated through the authorized process. The system may not approve or activate its own proposals. This decision does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.
- **AF-02 — Approved design decision:** Integrity verification failure, uncertainty, or unavailable trust material blocks all protected operations (Fail Closed). No automatic fallback to older, alternate, cached, or unverified policy is permitted. Recovery requires owner-directed manual restoration and independent re-verification. This decision does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.
- **AF-05 — Approved design decision:** UTC is the canonical internal storage and interchange representation for system event timestamps. RFC 3339 UTC serialization with `Z` is the standard representation; exact source precision is preserved without silent rounding or fabricated precision. Clock source and known synchronization/uncertainty metadata are recorded; unknown or unreliable clock quality is never represented as reliable. Untrusted wall-clock time cannot determine expiry or time-bound authorization; inability to establish trustworthy time results in Deny. Event IDs are unique, random, non-semantic and contain no personal data or embedded time. Monotonic clocks are used only for elapsed durations; if unavailable, duration measurement is marked unreliable rather than silently substituted with wall-clock time for security-sensitive measurements. Event metadata retention is deferred to a separately reviewed audit/privacy policy, not blanket indefinite retention. Local time is display-only. This decision does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.
- **AF-06 — Approved design decision:** File deletions create durable historical tombstones recording the file identity and last-known path, deletion timestamp in canonical UTC, and a reference to the actor and action that caused the deletion; the last-known version or digest is included when available. Tombstones do not retain deleted file contents and must not be silently expired or removed. SHA-256 is the initial supported content-digest algorithm, with the algorithm identifier stored alongside each digest. Repository-relative paths use forward-slash separators and preserve case; escaping or ambiguous paths are rejected. Renames are represented as lineage relationships when continuity can be established. Binary files are tracked by digest and metadata without unnecessary content duplication. Actor references use stable internal identifiers and minimize personal data. Any future retention, redaction, or purge mechanism requires a separately reviewed and approved policy addressing privacy, legal, security, and audit requirements. This decision does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.
- **AF-07 — Approved design decision:** Detected secrets and credentials are blocked by default. Redaction requires explicit, narrowly scoped authorization and successful post-redaction validation. Detection covers credentials, API tokens, private keys, passwords, session tokens, and recognizable personal data, using pattern and contextual methods where applicable. Uncertain detections and scanner failures block the protected operation. Detection rules are versioned; findings and diagnostics do not expose secret values and retain only minimum safe evidence. This decision does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.
- **AF-08 — Approved design decision:** Every write, execute, delete and publish action requires its own explicit, single-use approval bound to the actor, exact action, resource, purpose, relevant context and expiry, checked immediately before the side effect. Actors require unique, verifiable identities; roles define maximum authority but do not replace action-specific approval. Revocation invalidates approval immediately. Replay, mismatch, expiry, ambiguity or inability to verify authorization results in Deny. Approval, authorization decision and execution result are distinct audit events. This decision does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.
- **AF-09 — Approved design decision:** There is exactly one authoritative approved policy source for protected decisions. The Master Control System must identify and verify that source; it must not merge, choose between, or silently substitute competing policy sources. Authorization outcomes use a fixed, versioned reason-code taxonomy. Conflicts between policy sources, versions or applicable rules result in Deny for the affected protected operation until resolved through the owner-controlled process. The policy publication/update mechanism, actor identity lifecycle, exact reason-code catalogue and proposal evidence remain open. This decision does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.
- **AF-10 — Approved high-level design direction:** SHA-256 policy-content digests, Ed25519 signatures, detached JWS using EdDSA, a JCS-canonicalized manifest, an independently controlled read-only runtime trust store and rollback-resistant state. Owner-directed manual recovery is mandatory; hardware-backed, non-exportable owner key custody is preferred. Exact serialization/profile, manifest schema, digest coverage, signing ceremony, key lifecycle/custody/recovery and trust-store/anti-rollback implementation remain open. This decision does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.
- **AF-11 — Approved design decision:** Preserve exact source timestamp precision; record clock source and known synchronization status without overstating clock reliability; deny time-bound protected actions when trustworthy time cannot be established; use unique random non-semantic event IDs without personal data or embedded time; use monotonic clocks only for elapsed durations and mark measurements unreliable when unavailable rather than silently substituting wall-clock time for security-sensitive durations; defer metadata retention to a separately reviewed audit/privacy policy. This decision does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.
- **AF-12 — Approved design decision:** File identity is based on the exact path representation from the authoritative repository source, without case folding, Unicode normalization, or lossy rewriting. Preserve original path bytes when the source exposes them; where only decoded text is available, retain the exact source string and encoding context. If lossless identity or round-trip equivalence cannot be established, treat the path as ambiguous and block identity-sensitive changes. Non-UTF-8 paths, where supported, remain opaque identities with safe reversible display encoding. A platform unable to distinguish or faithfully represent paths must block potentially colliding writes, renames, and deletions. This decision does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.
- **AF-13 — Approved design decision:** File integrity requires an independently protected reference or trust anchor; a digest stored only beside the file or in the same mutable trust domain is insufficient. Digest-algorithm migration must be explicit and versioned, preserve historical algorithm identifiers and digests, and establish a verifiable link between old and new digests for the same exact file version. Unverifiable anchors or migration linkage result in an unverified integrity state and block integrity-dependent protected operations. This decision does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.
- **AF-14 — Approved design decision:** A rename is linked to an existing file lineage only when evidence supports continuity. Names, directory locations, and file sizes alone are insufficient. An identical cryptographic digest is strong evidence of content equality but not conclusive proof of identity. Changed-content renames require additional reliable evidence; ambiguous multiple-candidate matches are not automatically linked. The prior and new exact path identities remain separately recorded. Evidence, actor or authoritative repository event, and assessment outcome are retained. Insufficient, ambiguous, unavailable, or unverifiable evidence results in an unverified relationship; protected operations depending on that identity relationship fail closed. Reassessment adds a new attributable event and does not erase the prior assessment. This decision does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.

- **AF-15 — Approved design decision:** Each deletion creates a distinct append-only tombstone with a unique non-semantic ID, stable repository ID, exact file identity and last-known path, canonical UTC deletion timestamp with source precision/clock-quality context, stable minimal actor reference, verifiable reference to the specific authorized deletion action, last-known version and digest when available with explicit digest-algorithm identifier, independent integrity-evidence reference, and logical record-schema version. Unavailable information is explicitly marked unknown/not available rather than guessed. Tombstones contain metadata/references only, not deleted file contents. Corrections, supplemental evidence, reassessments, and schema migrations are separately attributable and must not overwrite or erase the original record. Automatic expiry or silent deletion is prohibited; exceptional redaction or purge requires separately reviewed and approved policy. Storage/serialization, integrity-anchor mechanism, actor-reference lifecycle, access controls, and exceptional redaction/purge procedure remain open. This decision does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.
- **AF-16 — Approved design decision:** Every actor reference uses a unique, stable, non-reassignable internal actor ID distinct from login names, display names, email addresses, and other personal data. Identity attributes are separated from historical references; deactivation/revocation blocks future access but does not erase historical references; IDs are never reassigned. Unresolvable actors remain unresolved and protected operations fail closed. This does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.
- **AF-17 — Approved design decision:** Tombstone access is deny-by-default and least-privilege. Read, export, annotate/reassess, and administer are distinct capabilities. Access and administrative actions are separately audited with minimal necessary evidence. Authorization is rechecked at action time; revocation is immediate; missing or unverifiable authorization results in Deny. Role catalogue and implementation remain open. This does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.
- **AF-18 — Approved design decision:** Tombstones remain append-only with no automatic expiry or silent deletion. Exceptional redaction/purge requires a separately reviewed and owner-approved policy and its own single-use, narrowly bound approval checked immediately before action. Prefer minimum-scope redaction over purge where adequate; preserve non-sensitive accountability evidence without copying removed sensitive values. Uncertain legal basis, authority, scope, or integrity impact results in Deny. The detailed exceptional policy remains open. This does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.

- **AF-19 — Approved design decision:** Integrity evidence must be anchored in a trust domain independently protected from the data it verifies. Colocated or same-mutable-domain digests are not independent anchors. Anchor changes require separate authorization and audit evidence. Loss, compromise, or unverifiable independence makes integrity unverified and blocks dependent protected operations; no automatic fallback is permitted. Anchor location, isolation, custody, availability, and recovery remain open. This does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.
- **AF-20 — Approved design decision:** Verification binds the exact file/version identity and digest to an independently protected reference and records algorithm, reference identity/version, time and clock quality, verifier, outcome, and evidence. Verified, failed, and indeterminate/unavailable are distinct outcomes. Missing, conflicting, invalid, or unavailable evidence results in Deny for dependent protected operations; a prior success cannot override a later failure. Protocol, evidence schema, freshness, and recovery details remain open. This does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.
- **AF-21 — Approved design decision:** Digest migration is explicit, versioned, separately approved, attributable, and never silently replaces historical evidence. Preserve algorithm identifiers and old digests; independently prove old/new digests refer to the same exact file identity and version. Record method, actor/process, time/clock quality, both algorithms/digests, evidence, and outcome. Unverifiable source evidence or linkage means unverified integrity and Deny for dependent actions. SHA-256 remains the initial algorithm; no successor or implementation is selected. This does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.


- **AF-22 — Approved design decision:** Before identity-sensitive file operations, assess whether the actual source/target platform, filesystem, repository interface, and operation can represent and distinguish exact path identities. Cover syntax, reserved names, case collisions, Unicode/encoding round trips, and relevant length constraints. Unknown capability or possible identity collision blocks writes, renames, and deletes before side effects; no silent normalization or overwrite. Capability method and platform matrix remain open. This does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.
- **AF-23 — Approved design decision:** Display/export paths are presentation only and never replace authoritative identity. Encoded forms for unusual/non-UTF-8 paths must be deterministic, unambiguous, lossless, reversible, and carry encoding context. Unsafe or non-reversible rendering leaves identity opaque and blocks operations requiring reconstruction from display text. Exact encoding grammar and interoperability tests remain open. This does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.
- **AF-24 — Approved design decision:** Rename assessment prioritizes authoritative repository-native history and verifiable source-control metadata, corroborated by independently verifiable content/version evidence. Weak contextual signals cannot prove identity; a numeric confidence score alone cannot authorize linkage. Record sources, versions, exact paths, candidates, actor/event, rule-set version, outcome, and reason. Require a uniquely supported relationship; ambiguity, contradiction, stale or missing evidence yields unverified and Deny for dependent protected operations. Reassessment is append-only. Detailed precedence, schema, and thresholds remain open. This does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.


- **AF-25 — Approved design decision:** The independent integrity anchor has a controlled, attributable lifecycle covering establishment, activation, change, rotation, suspension, compromise, revocation, and recovery. Each transition requires a separately authorized action bound to the exact anchor and transition. Only an active, independently verified anchor may support integrity decisions. Unavailable, conflicting, or potentially compromised state makes dependent evidence unverified and blocks protected operations. Recovery is owner-directed, preserves prior history, and requires fresh independent verification; no automatic fallback or silent replacement. Topology, custody, availability targets, rotation cadence, and detailed recovery remain open. This does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.
- **AF-26 — Approved design decision:** Every verification creates an immutable attributable evidence record bound to exact subject identity/version, digest/algorithm, anchor identity/version/state, verifier, timestamp/clock quality, protocol/rule-set version, outcome, reason, and evidence references. Outcomes are exactly VERIFIED, FAILED, or INDETERMINATE. Evidence applies only to the checked subject/version and trust context; it becomes stale on subject-version, anchor-trust/state, relevant protocol/rule changes, or revocation/contradiction. No fixed time-to-live is approved. Later failures/indeterminate outcomes remain separately recorded and cannot be overwritten by earlier success. Missing, stale, failed, or indeterminate evidence fails closed. This does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.
- **AF-27 — Approved design decision:** Tombstone access is deny-by-default with distinct read, export, review/annotate, and administer capabilities; grants are minimum-scope and purpose-bound, with no implied capability inheritance. Export requires privacy-filter validation and action-specific approval bound to dataset, destination, purpose, and context. Review may append evidence but cannot rewrite/delete originals. Administration cannot alter historical records or bypass integrity/redaction controls. Sensitive admin actions require separate AF-08 approval. Access and privileged actions are audited; authorization is rechecked immediately before action and revocation is immediate. Role mapping, separation of duties, delegation, and access-review cadence remain open. This does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.

- **AF-28 — Approved design decision:** Tombstone storage must preserve each original record as a durable append-only historical object; storage and serialization must not permit silent replacement, truncation, or deletion. A record is not considered successfully committed until the complete record and its required integrity evidence/references have been durably accepted and independently verifiable. Interrupted, partial, conflicting, or indeterminate persistence must be reported as incomplete/unverified and must not produce a false success state; dependent protected operations fail closed. Serialization must preserve exact logical field values, explicit unknown/unavailable states, identifiers, and schema version without lossy normalization. Storage technology, physical layout, transaction/replication guarantees, and serialization format remain open. This does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.
- **AF-29 — Approved design decision:** Every tombstone and schema-related event identifies the logical schema version it uses. Schema changes are explicit, documented, versioned, and attributable. Existing historical records and their original serialized representations remain preserved; any transformation, migration, or compatibility interpretation is performed as a separately attributable operation and does not overwrite the source record. Unknown or unsupported required fields, incompatible versions, lossy conversion, or inability to establish semantic equivalence results in an unsupported/unverified state and blocks dependent protected operations. Readers must not silently assume compatibility or discard unknown required data. Backward-compatibility policy, supported-version window, migration sequencing, and exact schema registry/format remain open. This does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.
- **AF-30 — Approved design decision:** Tombstones link to supporting evidence and audit events through stable, unique, verifiable references that identify the referenced object type and exact record/event version where applicable. References preserve provenance and must be independently resolvable and integrity-checkable; evidence contents are not copied into tombstones unless separately justified and approved. Missing, broken, ambiguous, conflicting, stale, or unverifiable references are explicitly marked unavailable/indeterminate and cannot be treated as proof; dependent protected operations fail closed. Later evidence or corrected links are appended as separately attributable records and never silently replace prior references or outcomes. ## Additional approved design decisions — Master Prompt Protection and Integrity Validation

The owner approved the following five proposals on 2026-11-14. These decisions refine AF-10 but do not select a vendor or implementation, approve the Master Prompt 0.1.0-draft, authorize implementation or key generation, or approve release.

- **AF-48 — Approved design decision:** The integrity manifest identifies the exact Master Prompt artifact and its verification context. Required logical fields are manifest schema version, document identity, Master Prompt version, digest algorithm and digest, signature algorithm, signing key identifier, publication timestamp, and prior approved version/reference where one exists. Required fields must be present, unambiguous, and validated; no missing values are inferred. Exact field names, types, byte-coverage rules, and serialization profile remain open.
- **AF-49 — Approved design decision:** Signature verification follows AF-10: Ed25519 with EdDSA in a detached JWS and SHA-256 policy-content digesting, with a JCS-canonicalized manifest. Protected headers and algorithm identifiers are strictly validated; unknown/disallowed algorithms, malformed signatures, unexpected critical headers, invalid canonicalization, and downgrade attempts are rejected. No automatic algorithm substitution or fallback verification method is permitted. Exact interoperable JWS profile and header allowlist remain open.
- **AF-50 — Approved design decision:** A policy signing ceremony is owner-controlled and binds approval and signature to the exact reviewed artifact/version: prepare candidate; freeze and identify exact contents; obtain explicit owner approval; compute approved digest; sign manifest through the separately controlled owner signing process; independently verify signature and artifact binding; then publish. The system may prepare and verify evidence but may not approve or sign for the owner. Any content change invalidates that approval/signature binding and requires a new review.
- **AF-51 — Approved design decision:** Signing keys follow a controlled, attributable lifecycle covering generation, registration, activation, rotation, suspension, compromise, revocation, and recovery. Hardware-backed, non-exportable owner key custody remains preferred. Private keys, recovery material, and secrets must never be stored in the repository, ordinary configuration, or logs. Runtime components cannot generate, export, replace, or use owner signing keys to approve/sign policy. Uncertain or compromised key status blocks dependent protected operations. Exact custody, ceremony, backup/recovery, rotation criteria, and separation-of-duties details remain open. This does not authorize key generation.
- **AF-52 — Approved design decision:** Runtime trust material is independently controlled and read-only to runtime components. Anti-rollback state must prevent silent activation of an older or downgraded policy and be updated in a controlled, integrity-protected manner. Missing, stale, conflicting, unavailable, or unverifiable trust/rollback state blocks protected operations; no automatic fallback to cached, alternate, or older policy is permitted. Recovery is owner-directed manual restoration followed by independent verification. Trust-store provisioning, anti-rollback representation, atomicity, durability, and recovery evidence remain open.

## Additional approved design decisions — repository revision and object history

The owner approved the following four refinements on 2026-11-14. The proposal to pin every scan or assessment to an exact repository snapshot is already covered by AF-36, AF-37 and AF-39; it is not duplicated as a new decision. These refinements do not authorize implementation, local-disk scanning, external storage access, or release.

- **AF-58 — Approved design decision:** When an explicitly selected source exposes both a working-copy state and a repository-committed version, observations of uncommitted or working-copy changes must be distinguished from committed repository history. A working-copy observation must not be represented as part of a commit unless that exact content is verifiably included in the commit. The component must not inspect local disks or additional repositories by inference; scope remains limited to the explicitly authorized source.
- **AF-59 — Approved design decision:** When repository history is rewritten, replaced, or made unavailable, preserve the previously observed history references and the newly observed state as distinct, attributable observations when evidence is available. Do not infer that the new history is equivalent to, or a continuation of, the prior history without verifiable evidence. Missing prior objects or insufficient evidence must be reported as unavailable or indeterminate; no history is silently discarded or reconstructed.
- **AF-60 — Approved design decision:** A merge observation preserves references to each verifiable parent/source version and the resulting version, including the relationship between them. Conflict occurrence and resolution are recorded as attributable events when exposed by the authoritative source. Conflict resolution is never inferred, silently flattened into an ordinary change, or performed automatically by this component.
- **AF-61 — Approved design decision:** Repository objects with distinct semantics, including symbolic links, submodules, and references to externally managed content, must be identified as such rather than silently treated as ordinary file contents. Record only the object metadata and verifiable target/version reference available from the explicitly selected repository source. Do not dereference links, inspect external storage, or fetch another repository unless that exact access is separately authorized. If object identity, target, or version cannot be established, mark it unknown or unverified rather than guessing.
