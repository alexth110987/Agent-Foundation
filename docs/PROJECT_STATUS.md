## Recorded design decisions
- **AF-01 — Approved design decision:** The Master Control System may propose additional policy rules, but proposals remain non-operative until the owner explicitly approves them and the approved policy source is updated through the authorized process. The system may not approve or activate its own proposals. This decision does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.
- **AF-02 — Approved design decision:** Integrity verification failure, uncertainty, or unavailable trust material blocks all protected operations (Fail Closed). No automatic fallback to older, alternate, cached, or unverified policy is permitted. Recovery requires owner-directed manual restoration and independent re-verification. This decision does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.
- **AF-05 — Approved design decision:** UTC is the canonical internal storage and interchange representation for system event timestamps. RFC 3339 UTC serialization with `Z` is the standard representation; exact source precision is preserved without silent rounding or fabricated precision. Clock source and known synchronization/uncertainty metadata are recorded; unknown or unreliable clock quality is never represented as reliable. Untrusted wall-clock time cannot determine expiry or time-bound authorization; inability to establish trustworthy time results in Deny. Event IDs are unique, random, non-semantic and contain no personal data or embedded time. Monotonic clocks are used only for elapsed durations; if unavailable, duration measurement is marked unreliable rather than silently substituted with wall-clock time for security-sensitive measurements. Event metadata retention is deferred to a separately reviewed audit/privacy policy, not blanket indefinite retention. Local time is display-only. This decision does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.
- **AF-06 — Approved design decision:** File deletions create durable historical tombstones recording the file identity and last-known path, deletion timestamp in canonical UTC, and a reference to the actor and action that caused the deletion; the last-known version or digest is included when available. Tombstones do not retain deleted file contents and must not be silently expired or removed. SHA-256 is the initial supported content-digest algorithm, with the algorithm identifier stored alongside each digest. Repository-relative paths use forward-slash separators and preserve case; escaping or ambiguous paths are rejected. Renames are represented as lineage relationships when continuity can be established. Binary files are tracked by digest and metadata without unnecessary content duplication. Actor references use stable internal identifiers and minimize personal data. Any future retention, redaction, or purge mechanism requires a separately reviewed and approved policy addressing privacy, legal, security, and audit requirements. This decision does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.
- **AF-07 — Approved design decision:** Detected secrets and credentials are blocked by default. Redaction requires explicit, narrowly scoped authorization and successful post-redaction validation. Detection covers credentials, API tokens, private keys, passwords, session tokens, and recognizable personal data, using pattern and contextual methods where applicable. Uncertain detections and scanner failures block the protected operation. Detection rules are versioned; findings and diagnostics do not expose secret values and retain only minimum safe evidence. This decision does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.
- **AF-08 — Approved design decision:** Every write, execute, delete and publish action requires its own explicit, single-use approval bound to the actor, exact action, resource, purpose, relevant context and expiry, checked immediately before the side effect. Actors require unique, verifiable identities; roles define maximum authority but do not replace action-specific approval. Revocation invalidates approval immediately. Replay, mismatch, expiry, ambiguity or inability to verify authorization results in Deny. Approval, authorization decision and execution result are distinct audit events. This decision does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.
- **AF-09 — Approved design decision:** There is exactly one authoritative approved policy source for protected decisions. The Master Control System must identify and verify that source; it must not merge, choose between, or silently substitute competing policy sources. Authorization outcomes use a fixed, versioned reason-code taxonomy. Conflicts between policy sources, versions or applicable rules result in Deny for the affected protected operation until resolved through the owner-controlled process. The policy publication/update mechanism, actor identity lifecycle, exact reason-code catalogue and proposal evidence remain open. This decision does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.
- **AF-10 — Approved high-level design direction:** SHA-256 policy-content digests, Ed25519 signatures, detached JWS using EdDSA, a JCS-canonicalized manifest, an independently controlled read-only runtime trust store and rollback-resistant state. This decision does not approve the Master Prompt draft or authorize implementation.
- **AF-11 — Approved design decision:** Preserve exact source timestamp precision; record clock source and known synchronization status without overstating clock reliability; deny time-bound protected actions when trustworthy time cannot be established; use unique random non-semantic event IDs without personal data or embedded time; use monotonic clocks only for elapsed durations and mark measurements unreliable when unavailable rather than silently substituting wall-clock time for security-sensitive durations; defer metadata retention to a separately reviewed audit/privacy policy. - **AF-12 — Approved design decision:** File identity is based on the exact path representation from the authoritative repository source, without case folding, Unicode normalization, or lossy rewriting. Uninspected or inaccessible relevant metadata is reported as unknown/incomplete; the content must not be described as fully sanitized, and dependent protected transfers fail closed where sensitive metadata may remain. This does not authorize access beyond the selected content or approve implementation.
- **AF-69 — Approved design decision:** Encoded, compressed, or obfuscated content is not presumed clean merely because ordinary text or pattern scanning finds no match. Inspection is limited to explicitly supported and safely inspectable representations. If relevant content cannot be decoded or inspected safely, record that limitation as unknown/incomplete and block the dependent protected transfer. No unsafe decoding, execution, or automatic interpretation is authorized. This does not approve implementation.
- **AF-70 — Approved design decision:** Nested files and archives are inspected only within explicit, approved nesting-depth, expanded-size, and processing limits. The scan records applicable limits and whether the full relevant content was covered. If a limit, parser failure, or inaccessible member prevents complete inspection, the result is incomplete rather than clean; dependent protected transfers fail closed where uninspected content may contain sensitive material. This does not authorize unbounded extraction or approve implementation.
- **AF-71 — Approved design decision:** A positive privacy-scan result is bound to the exact content/version, destination, purpose, and detection-rule-set version assessed. Any change to one of these dimensions requires a new assessment before protected transfer or publication; a prior positive result must not be reused for a different transfer context. If the exact binding or current applicability cannot be verified, the result is indeterminate and the protected operation is blocked. This does not approve implementation.

## Additional approved design decisions — Security and Permission Control

The owner approved the following five refinements on 2026-11-14. These are design decisions only; they do not approve the Master Prompt 0.1.0-draft, authorize implementation, select an identity provider or technology, or approve release.

- **AF-72 — Approved design decision:** Changes to roles, permissions, and actor-to-role assignments are themselves protected administrative actions. They require explicit authorization, attributable evidence, and validation against the current policy immediately before taking effect. An actor must not use a permission-management action to expand their own authority or bypass an applicable restriction. Any unresolved authority, scope, or policy conflict results in Deny. This does not approve implementation.
- **AF-73 — Approved design decision:** Delegated authority, where separately permitted by policy, must be explicitly bounded to the delegating actor, delegate, permitted action, resource, purpose, context, and validity period. Delegation cannot exceed the delegator's current authority, cannot convert a restricted or non-delegable permission into a delegable one, and cannot be re-delegated unless an explicit approved rule permits it. Missing, ambiguous, expired, or unverifiable delegation evidence results in Deny. This does not establish that delegation is currently enabled or approve implementation.
- **AF-74 — Approved design decision:** Revocation of an identity, role, permission, delegation, or approval must prevent subsequent protected actions using that authority. Authorization must use current, verifiable revocation state at the action boundary. If timely revocation state cannot be established, the protected action is denied; stale cached authorization must not be used as a fallback. This does not select a propagation mechanism or measurable service-level target, or approve implementation.
- **AF-75 — Approved design decision:** Read access is independently scoped by actor, resource, data category, purpose, and permitted read operation. Access to one resource or data category does not imply access to related resources, metadata, search results, exports, or other representations. Read, search, inspect, and export remain distinguishable where their exposure differs. Ambiguous or excessive scope is denied. This does not approve implementation.
- **AF-76 — Approved design decision:** A single-use approval must be consumed atomically with its successful authorization at the protected action boundary, so concurrent or replayed requests cannot both use the same approval. If the system cannot establish whether an approval was already consumed, or cannot guarantee one-time use for the action, it must deny the protected operation rather than risk duplicate execution. This does not select a nonce/token mechanism or transaction technology, or approve implementation.

## Additional approved design decisions — GitHub Integration

The owner approved the following five design decisions on 2026-11-15. They refine component 10 only; they do not approve the Master Prompt 0.1.0-draft, authorize implementation, grant GitHub permissions, select a vendor/API version/technology, enable external integrations or autonomous writes, or approve release.

- **AF-82 — Approved design decision:** Each operation is bound to one explicitly selected GitHub repository and its verifiable, stable GitHub Repository ID; owner/name strings alone are insufficient identity evidence. Current owner/name and canonical repository URL are retained as locators, not as the sole identity. Rename or transfer observations are recorded as attributable changes and trigger re-verification of repository identity, access scope, permissions, and applicable policy before further protected actions. Ambiguous, changed, unavailable, or unverifiable identity results in Deny for identity-dependent operations. No other repository may be accessed by inference.
- **AF-83 — Approved design decision:** GitHub authority is scoped per operation and resource, with read, write, delete, merge, workflow-dispatch, administration, and other materially different capabilities kept distinct. A granted read capability never implies write or execution authority. The requested operation must fit both the actor's maximum permitted authority and the exact resource scope; least privilege and deny-by-default apply. Required permissions must be verified at the action boundary, and missing, excessive, ambiguous, stale, or unverifiable permission evidence results in Deny. Each protected side effect also requires the separate single-use action approval required by AF-08. Tokens, credentials, and secrets are never persisted in repository content, ordinary configuration, or logs.
- **AF-84 — Approved design decision:** Every supported GitHub API operation must be associated with an explicit, versioned API/request contract defining its endpoint and method, required inputs, authorization scope, expected response structure, required fields and types, and relevant error outcomes. The API version and contract version used are recorded with minimal safe request/response metadata. Responses are validated before interpretation; unknown fields may be ignored only where the contract explicitly permits this, while missing required fields, incompatible types, unsupported response versions, malformed data, and incomplete/error responses must not be interpreted as success. Unrecognized or unverifiable responses produce BLOCKED or INDETERMINATE as appropriate. Sensitive payloads and credentials are excluded from evidence.
- **AF-85 — Approved design decision:** Paginated GitHub results must expose and record their coverage, including the pagination mechanism and the pages/cursors actually processed, applicable limits, and whether traversal completed. Interrupted, capped, inconsistent, or otherwise incomplete traversal is explicitly marked incomplete; absence from an incomplete result set is not evidence of absence. Where repository state can change during traversal, snapshot/consistency limitations are recorded and conclusions requiring a stable complete view are marked indeterminate unless that view can be established. Protected operations requiring complete coverage are blocked when completeness cannot be verified. No unbounded pagination or inferred completeness is permitted.
- **AF-86 — Approved design decision:** GitHub API failures, rate limits, conflicts, and uncertain mutation outcomes are handled explicitly and fail closed where outcome or authorization is uncertain. Retries are bounded and must respect server-provided retry guidance and configured limits; automatic retry may be used only for operations classified as safe and repeatable by their approved contract. A write, merge, deletion, or workflow dispatch with an uncertain outcome must never be blindly repeated. The current repository/ref/resource state and expected branch head must first be re-fetched and reconciled; if the outcome remains ambiguous, stop and report INDETERMINATE for owner-directed resolution. Branch protection must be respected; no bypass, force update, or automatic conflict resolution is permitted. These rules refine, and do not weaken, the global expected-head and authorization safeguards.



## Additional approved design decision — Privacy Filter trust-boundary inspection

The owner approved the following design decision on 2026-11-15. It refines component 07 only; it does not approve the Master Prompt 0.1.0-draft, authorize implementation, expand source access, or approve release.

- **AF-87 — Approved design decision:** Apply routine privacy inspection at trust-boundary crossings, not to every internal operation. Inspect incoming data when it enters the controlled environment from an external or otherwise untrusted source, before it is admitted to protected internal workflows. Inspect outgoing data before it crosses from the controlled environment to an external, untrusted, or otherwise unapproved destination. Internal processing, editing, copying, renaming, and movement between resources that remain within the same explicitly controlled trust boundary do not, by themselves, trigger a new privacy scan. This exemption is limited to the internal operation: it does not waive the required inspection of a later outbound transfer. This decision does not exempt secret handling, access control, integrity verification, malware/security controls, authorization, or other applicable safeguards; it does not approve implementation or expand the 18-component scope.
